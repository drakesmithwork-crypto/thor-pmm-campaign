Why schools need better first-minute visibility

During critical incidents, schools and responders often face fragmented information, delayed context, and difficult coordination decisions. Thor is positioned to close that visibility gap, so response teams can act with clearer information in the moments that matter most.

Current challenge

Alerts often arrive before enough context exists to guide high-confidence decisions.

Response gap

Fixed cameras and manual updates can leave blind spots in fast-evolving events.

Thor focus

Enable faster, trusted situational awareness while preserving accountability and privacy limits.

What Thor is, and what it is not

Thor is a remote-pilot-app-led emergency situational awareness capability for verified critical incidents. It is designed to support authorized human decision-makers, not replace them.

Thor helps with

  • Incident-limited aerial visibility for authorized responders
  • Structured hand-offs between school teams, GSOC, and dispatch
  • Audit-ready decision timelines for after-action accountability
  • Integrated view across alerts, cameras, drone, and response status

Thor is not used for

  • Routine student monitoring or broad surveillance
  • Unrestricted video access by non-authorized roles
  • Automated policing decisions without human review
  • Always-on campus tracking

How it works: incident-to-end-of-call flow

ConOps (Concept of Operations) defines the sequence and hand-offs from detection through closure. Each major transition includes a responsible human role, system action, and policy checkpoint.

  1. Incident detected: School staff trigger approved emergency protocol.
  2. Alert correlation: Alert and camera context are correlated for rapid validation.
  3. GSOC review: Human operator validates signal and urgency.
  4. Launch decision: Authorized operator approves incident-qualified deployment in remote pilot app.
  5. Live visibility: Drone and camera context are shared with authorized responders.
  6. Dispatch coordination: Verified context is communicated to public safety dispatch.
  7. Stabilization: Incident transitions to controlled close-out.
  8. Governance workflow: Retention, audit, and after-action reviews are executed.

Privacy, ethics, and accountability

Purpose limitation

Thor is scoped to verified critical incidents and emergency response support.

Role-based access

Only authorized roles can access incident media, with auditable access logs.

Time-bound retention

Incident data follows predefined retention and deletion workflows.

Human governance

Activation, escalation, and dispatch actions are human-supervised decisions.

Policy commitment

Campaign language and operational behavior are aligned to one rule: safety outcomes do not require sacrificing student privacy or community trust.

Community FAQ

Top trust and privacy concerns, answered in plain language.

Is Thor always recording students?

No. Thor is positioned for verified critical incidents and not routine daily monitoring.

Who can view incident footage?

Only authorized roles under district policy and legal requirements, with access audit trails.

Can Thor dispatch police automatically?

No. Dispatch decisions remain with authorized human operators and established protocols.

How long is incident footage stored?

Retention is policy-defined, time-bound, and subject to legal/compliance requirements.

How will families get transparency on system use?

District policy summaries, governance ownership, and community communication channels are required.

Stakeholder value by audience

District and board priorities

  • Improve emergency decision quality with clearer context.
  • Strengthen governance and accountability with auditable workflows.
  • Evaluate readiness through measurable pilot criteria.

Parent and educator priorities

  • Incident-limited use with explicit non-surveillance boundaries.
  • Transparency on who can access data and why.
  • Clear policy protections for privacy and retention limits.

Public safety and response priorities

  • Faster visibility before arrival for safer tactical decisions.
  • Structured dispatch hand-offs with confidence indicators.
  • Shared after-action timeline for continuous improvement.

Pilot and readiness framework

Pilot design should be governance-first: policy approval, role training, engineering claim validation, and communication readiness before public rollout.

Phase A: Internal readiness

  • Validate all claims with engineering and policy teams
  • Finalize guardrails and spokesperson messaging
  • Complete operator and district training

Phase B: Leadership alignment

  • Board briefings and operational walkthroughs
  • Readiness scorecard and risk review
  • Approval of pilot scope and reporting cadence

Phase C: Community trust rollout

  • Parent and educator FAQ distribution
  • Town-hall communications and feedback loop
  • Transparent progress and lessons-learned updates
