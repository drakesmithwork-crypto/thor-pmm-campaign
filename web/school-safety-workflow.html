The Desert Mountain Incident — Capstone Presentation

A Narrative for the Axon Drone Capstone

Desert Mountain High School, Scottsdale — large single-story campus, ~1,400 students. Five TASER-equipped drones in locked ceiling docks. None have ever fired. None have ever recorded a single second of footage. Fifteen miles away at Scottsdale PD, RSOC operator Torres is on duty. His queue is empty. The system is working exactly as designed.

Coherent chain of command

An alert reaches local PD through infrastructure they already trust — 911 call, ShotSpotter, staff panic wearable, or SRO radio. No Axon cameras required for the trigger. Most deaths in a school shooting happen in the first two minutes; the dock enables sub-two-minute response without permanent surveillance. Once the alert hits the RTCC, a watch commander authorizes deploy — it is never automatic. Policy must draw the line: authorized emergency response vs. prohibited general surveillance. This implementation satisfies that line by design.

Act 2 — Deployment (1:20)

Marsh approves Drone 4 only. Torres preflight → launch 9:48:20. Live feed to pilot app, Fusus, Respond within 5 sec. POI at corridor end — raises handgun.

Act 3 — TASER (2:10)

Hold → TASER arm request → Marsh approves → target lock → 2-sec hold to discharge. Subject down 9:49:41 — 2:41 after panic button.

Act 4 — Campus holds

While Drone 4 flew, Drones 1–3 and 5 did exactly nothing. Marsh deliberately did not launch others. 1,400 students in north, south, commons, and gym saw zero drones overhead. Response calibrated to threat — not a surveillance network.

Act 5 — Handoff (2:41)

Torres pushes incident package: subject down, weapon unsecured, overwatch active. Officers enter 9:51 with same feed. Subject secured 9:52. Five minutes after panic button.

Act 6 — Closure

Drone 4 docked 9:54. 11 minutes of footage — Drone 4 only. Drones 1–3, 5: zero footage. Full audit trail; incident-limited retention.

Open questions — named honestly

Misidentification

Hold state, second-human TASER approval, and 2-sec confirmation create friction — but do not eliminate risk if feed quality is poor or a student enters the corridor.

Legal framework

Armed drone deployment in schools requires policy that does not fully exist yet. FAA Part 108 still finalizing. Axon's 2022 ethics board episode shows the public conversation is not settled.

Equity

Who decides which schools get armed drones? Desert Mountain is a deliberately chosen pilot environment — framework must be proven before broader deployment.

The vision: faster than human response, more restrained than always-on surveillance, human chain of command at every gate, accountable to an immutable audit record. It does not exist in final form today — but Desert Mountain shows exactly what it would look like if it did.
