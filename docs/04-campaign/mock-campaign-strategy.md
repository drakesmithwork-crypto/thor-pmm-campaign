# Thor Mock Campaign Strategy

## Campaign Objective
Position Thor as a trusted, policy-governed emergency response support capability that helps schools and responders act faster during critical incidents without normalizing student surveillance.

## Campaign Success Criteria
- Stakeholders can explain what Thor is and what it is not.
- Privacy concerns are proactively addressed with policy-specific language.
- Decision-makers understand operational workflow and accountability.
- Sales and communications teams have a consistent mixed-audience narrative.

## Audience Framework

### Primary Decision Audience
- School boards and district administrators.
- Need: risk management, operational clarity, budget justification, policy defensibility.

### Trust-Critical Community Audience
- Parents, educators, student representatives.
- Need: privacy boundaries, human control, transparency, misuse prevention.

### Operational Partner Audience
- School safety teams, GSOC operations, public safety liaisons.
- Need: workflow readiness, role clarity, dispatch coordination evidence.

## Positioning Pillars
1. Faster situational clarity in critical moments.
2. Human-governed operations with explicit policy checkpoints.
3. Privacy guardrails and access accountability by design.
4. Unified school-to-responder coordination through integrated systems.

## Core Message House

### Master Narrative
Thor helps authorized responders gain trusted incident visibility quickly so schools can coordinate safer, faster decisions during critical events while maintaining strict privacy and governance boundaries.

### Message Layer: Board/Administrators
- "Improve emergency response readiness with integrated situational awareness."
- "Adopt a policy-governed workflow that documents accountability end to end."
- "Support duty of care with measurable operational readiness indicators."

### Message Layer: Parents/Educators
- "Built for critical incidents, not daily student monitoring."
- "Human decisions and role-based access controls remain central."
- "Clear retention, transparency, and oversight commitments are non-negotiable."

### Message Layer: Operational Stakeholders
- "Connect alerts, visual context, and dispatch hand-offs in one workflow."
- "Improve action quality in high-pressure decision windows."
- "Standardize incident documentation for after-action learning and compliance."

## Proof Point Framework (Claims Require Validation)
- Reduced time to validated situational awareness.
- Improved quality/consistency of incident hand-offs.
- Audit-complete decision trails during emergency workflows.
- Training readiness and protocol adherence metrics.

## Objection Handling Guide

### Objection: "This is surveillance technology in schools."
Response direction:
- Clarify incident-limited purpose and activation criteria.
- State explicit restrictions on routine monitoring.
- Reference published governance and oversight process.

### Objection: "Who gets to watch footage?"
Response direction:
- Explain role-based authorized access only.
- Explain audit logging and policy enforcement.
- Clarify retention/deletion controls.

### Objection: "Will this automate policing decisions?"
Response direction:
- Emphasize human-in-the-loop launch and dispatch decisions.
- Clarify that Thor supports, not replaces, established emergency protocols.

### Objection: "Can schools afford and operationalize this?"
Response direction:
- Focus on risk-reduction value and readiness outcomes.
- Present phased adoption and training approach.
- Tie deployment to clear governance milestones.

## Launch Sequencing (Mock)

### Phase A: Internal Readiness
- Validate all claims with SW/ME/legal.
- Train spokespersons on trust-sensitive language.
- Finalize objection handling and FAQs.

### Phase B: Leadership and Board Briefing
- Present board packet and ConOps walkthrough.
- Align on governance preconditions for pilot.

### Phase C: Community Trust Rollout
- Conduct parent/educator communications and town-hall sessions.
- Collect feedback and publish clarifications.

### Phase D: Pilot Readiness Narrative
- Communicate scope, constraints, and success metrics.
- Reinforce policy monitoring and transparent reporting cadence.

## Channel and Format Recommendations
- Executive briefings: concise, evidence-led presentations.
- Community channels: FAQ-driven, plain-language digital and print materials.
- Internal teams: playbook-style artifacts for consistency and training.

## Campaign Guardrails
- Do not use fear-based imagery or language.
- Do not imply universal threat detection capability.
- Do not overstate autonomy or operational certainty.
- Do not launch messaging before policy and engineering validation.

## KPI Framework (Mock)
- Message clarity score from stakeholder testing.
- Trust acceptance indicators by audience segment.
- Objection resolution rate in board/community sessions.
- Readiness completion against governance checklist.
