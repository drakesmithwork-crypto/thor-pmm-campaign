# Week 01 Foundation: Community Advisory Board Framework for School Safety Technology

## Objective
Initiate a practical, trust-centered advisory board model for early community input on public school safety technology (students, parents, educators) before campaign finalization.

## Why Start Here
Advisory board research and public-sector guidance consistently emphasize that boards are most useful when they have:
- A clear charter and scope
- Defined operating procedures
- Balanced membership
- Baseline training/resources
- Transparent outputs to community and decision-makers

## Proposed Advisory Board Charter (Draft)

### Mission
Provide independent, community-grounded recommendations on the acceptable use, governance, communication, and accountability of school safety technology proposals.

### Scope (Phase 1)
- Review proposed use cases and non-use boundaries.
- Evaluate privacy and data-governance guardrails.
- Assess communication language for parent/student trust impact.
- Recommend transparency and oversight expectations.

### Out of scope (Phase 1)
- Procurement decisions
- Vendor selection authority
- Tactical law-enforcement command decisions

## Membership Model (Starter)
Total seats: 12-15

- Students: 2-3
- Parents/guardians: 3-4
- Educators/staff: 3-4
- School administrators/safety leads: 2
- Civil liberties/privacy advisor (external): 1
- Optional public safety liaison (non-voting): 1

Design notes:
- Ensure demographic and school-level diversity.
- Use fixed terms with staggered rotation.
- Publish role expectations and conflict disclosures.

## Operating Procedures (Starter)
- Meeting cadence: biweekly in first 8 weeks, then monthly.
- Decision method: documented recommendations with majority and minority notes.
- Agenda ownership: district/community liaison + board chair.
- Recordkeeping: published agendas, minutes, and recommendation summaries.
- Public input: open comment intake form with issue categories.

## Board Enablement Requirements
- Orientation module: privacy law basics, school safety policy, technology basics.
- Glossary and plain-language primers.
- Dedicated administrative support (agenda, notes, logistics).
- Access support (transport, timing, accessibility accommodations).

## First 30-Day Workplan

### Week 1-2
- Approve charter draft
- Confirm recruitment criteria
- Publish participation call and application form

### Week 3
- Seat members and conduct orientation
- Adopt meeting protocol and recommendation format

### Week 4
- Hold first policy-and-messaging review session
- Publish first advisory memo (top concerns + guardrail recommendations)

## Starter Question Set for First Meeting
1. What use cases feel clearly acceptable, conditionally acceptable, or unacceptable?
2. What boundaries are non-negotiable for student privacy?
3. What activation criteria are required for public trust?
4. What must be publicly disclosed before any pilot?
5. What language feels informative vs fear-inducing?

## Deliverables This Foundation Enables
- Audience-tested FAQ set
- Policy guardrail refinements
- Messaging red-lines and approved vocabulary
- Public transparency checklist for rollout readiness

## Success Criteria for the Advisory Board Pilot
- Participation across all core stakeholder groups
- Actionable recommendations produced in first 60 days
- Recommendations traceably integrated into policy/messaging artifacts
- Positive trend in trust/comprehension pulse checks
