# Thor Ethical Positioning Playbook

## Purpose
Provide a clear trust framework for students, parents, educators, and district leaders describing how Thor supports safety objectives while protecting privacy and civil liberties.

## Ethical Positioning Principles

1. Purpose Limitation
Thor is designated for verified critical safety incidents and emergency response support, not routine observation of students or staff.

2. Human Governance
All material activation, escalation, and dispatch-related decisions are made by authorized humans under policy-defined criteria.

3. Minimum Necessary Data
Only data needed to assess and respond to an active incident should be collected, accessed, and retained.

4. Role-Based Access
Incident media and related records are restricted to authorized roles with auditable access controls.

5. Time-Bound Retention
Retention periods are predefined by policy and legal requirements, with documented deletion workflows.

6. Transparency and Accountability
Districts maintain clear public-facing policy language, oversight ownership, and incident auditability.

## What Thor Does Not Do
- Does not run continuous always-on student surveillance.
- Does not perform unrestricted facial recognition for routine student tracking.
- Does not allow open-ended access to footage by unauthorized personnel.
- Does not replace school emergency plans, human judgment, or legal process.
- Does not auto-dispatch law enforcement without authorized human review.

## Activation and Operational Guardrails

### Activation Triggers (Policy Basis)
Thor activation should require a verified trigger category such as:
- Confirmed critical incident alert from approved systems.
- Authorized emergency request by designated school safety personnel.
- Escalation decision by GSOC/operator under documented criteria.

### Activation Exclusions
Thor should not be activated for:
- Routine disciplinary events.
- General student behavior monitoring.
- Non-safety administrative observation.

### Human-in-the-Loop Controls
- Named roles with activation authority.
- Secondary confirmation requirement for high-risk escalations (where feasible).
- Manual override and immediate stand-down process.

## Data Governance Framework

### Access Control
- Principle of least privilege by role.
- Access requests tied to incident identifiers.
- Mandatory audit log for every access event.

### Storage and Retention (Template Policy)
- Active incident media retained for a defined period (example placeholders pending legal review).
- Extended retention only for lawful investigatory, legal, or mandated compliance needs.
- Automatic deletion workflow with exception documentation.

### Sharing Rules
- External sharing limited to authorized legal/public safety channels.
- Parent/community communication uses policy summaries, not unrestricted raw incident media.

## Parent/Student/Educator FAQ

### Is Thor always recording students?
No. Thor is policy-scoped to verified critical incident support and is not intended for continuous routine monitoring.

### Who can view video from an incident?
Only authorized roles under district policy and legal requirements, with access tracked in audit logs.

### When can the drone be activated?
Activation follows predefined incident criteria and authorized human decision points.

### How long is footage kept?
Retention is time-bound by district policy and legal requirements, with deletion workflows and documented exceptions.

### Does Thor use facial recognition to track students?
Thor campaign and policy positioning should explicitly reject routine student tracking claims and define any analytics boundaries transparently.

### Can Thor dispatch police automatically?
No. Dispatch decisions remain with authorized human operators following established emergency protocols.

### How can families hold the system accountable?
Districts should publish governance policy, define oversight ownership, and provide channels for questions and policy review.

## Communication Guidelines for Trust-Critical Audiences

### Message Structure
1. Start with safety objective and incident-limited scope.
2. State explicit privacy boundaries.
3. Explain who decides and who can access data.
4. Clarify retention/deletion policy.
5. Reinforce transparency and accountability mechanisms.

### Tone Guidance
- Be specific and policy-backed; avoid vague assurances.
- Lead with constraints before capabilities.
- Use plain language, not technical jargon.
- Acknowledge concerns as valid and expected.

## Governance Roles (Template)
- District Policy Owner: approves operational/privacy policy.
- Safety Operations Lead: manages activation protocols.
- Data Steward/Compliance Lead: oversees retention and access audits.
- Community Liaison: manages family communication and trust feedback.

## Pre-Launch Ethics Readiness Checklist
- Activation criteria documented and approved.
- Access roles and audit logging configured.
- Retention/deletion schedule approved by legal/compliance.
- Family-facing FAQ and policy summary published.
- Oversight escalation path established.
- Staff training completed for authorized operators.

## Required Cross-Functional Validation
Before external messaging, validate final claims with:
- Product/Engineering (feature boundaries).
- Legal/Policy (compliance posture).
- Security/IT (access and logging controls).
- PMM/Comms (language clarity and trust impact).
